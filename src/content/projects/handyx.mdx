## Where it began

HandyX started from a simple observation: most of the stress of arranging a
service is not the service itself, but the silence in between. Did the request
go through? Has someone accepted it? Are they on the way? A good app should
answer those questions before people have to ask them.

## The awkward middle

Each feature was manageable on its own. The hard part was making them work
together on real phones, in real conditions:

- **Authentication** that stays out of the way, but never leaves someone
  logged into the wrong state.
- **Messaging** that survives a weak signal and doesn't lose what someone
  typed.
- **Maps and location** that are useful without being battery-hungry or
  intrusive.
- **Real-time updates** that reflect what is actually happening, not what
  happened thirty seconds ago.

Most of the effort went into the unglamorous paths: offline states, retries,
loading screens, and error messages written for someone in a hurry.

## What I learned

- Design the unhappy paths as carefully as the happy one. On mobile, poor
  connectivity is the normal case.
- Real-time features are mostly about honesty: showing when data is stale is
  better than pretending it is fresh.
- Shipping is its own skill. Builds, store reviews, and release notes deserve
  planning, not a Friday afternoon.
