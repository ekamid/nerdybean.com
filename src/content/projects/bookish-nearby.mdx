## Where it began

I have always liked the accident of finding a book in the right place:
reading about a city while walking through it, or picking up something from a
shelf in a café. Bookish Nearby was an attempt to design for that kind of
accident instead of optimising it away.

## The interesting problems

- **Serendipity versus relevance.** Pure recommendation is efficient and a
  little dull. Pure randomness is exciting and mostly useless. Most of the
  design work was finding a balance between the two.
- **Location as context, not surveillance.** The app needed to know roughly
  where you were without making that feel creepy. Coarse location and clear
  explanations went a long way.
- **Empty states.** In quiet areas there might be nothing nearby. The app had
  to fail gracefully and still suggest something worth reading.

## What I learned

It was the first project where understanding people mattered as much as
writing code. How someone chooses a book is personal and hard to predict, and
testing ideas with real readers was far more useful than guessing.
