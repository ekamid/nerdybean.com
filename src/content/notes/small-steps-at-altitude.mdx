intro: >-
  On a steep trail at altitude, the body renegotiates every plan you made at
  sea level. Breathing takes effort, sleep is thin, and walking uphill feels
  like carrying someone you didn't agree to carry. Nothing about it is
  heroic. Most of it is counting.
sections:
  - heading: Thin air, honest limits
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        Altitude removes the option of pretending. You cannot will your way
        through low oxygen; you can only go slowly enough for your body to keep
        up. The standard advice, climb high and sleep low, rest days, drink
        more than feels necessary, is not optional wisdom. Ignoring it is how
        trips end early.
      - >-
        What surprised me was how quickly the mind accepted the new scale.
        After a day or two I stopped thinking about the summit or the pass and
        started thinking about the next switchback, the next rest, the next cup
        of something hot.
  - heading: Progress you can't feel
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        Slow walking uphill feels like no progress at all. Then you turn around
        and the valley you left in the morning is a thin line far below. I
        think about that view whenever a piece of work feels stuck. Progress is
        often invisible at the scale of the step and obvious at the scale of
        the day.
  - heading: Turning around is a skill
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        The people I trusted most in the mountains were the ones most willing
        to say "not today". Weather changes, someone feels unwell, and the plan
        stops being sensible. Choosing to turn back while it is still your
        decision is not failure. It is the whole reason you get to come back.
pullQuote: >-
  The mountain doesn't care about your schedule. That is exactly why it is
  worth listening to.
