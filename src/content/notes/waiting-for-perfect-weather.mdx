intro: >-
  When I first moved to Cardiff, I checked the forecast before every run and
  postponed whenever it looked grim. After a few weeks I noticed I had
  postponed most of them. Wales is not a country that rewards waiting for the
  sky to cooperate.
sections:
  - heading: Conditions are never ideal
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        Rain in Wales is less an event than a setting. Once I accepted that, a
        good waterproof and a slightly lower standard for comfort changed
        everything. The run I actually do in drizzle beats the perfect run I
        keep planning for Saturday.
      - >-
        Most of the projects I have left unfinished were waiting for similar
        conditions: more time, a clearer idea, a better week. They never
        arrived, because they are not the kind of thing that arrives.
  - heading: Small, regular, slightly damp
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        The habits that stuck were the ones I made smaller than seemed
        worthwhile. Twenty minutes instead of an hour. One paragraph instead of
        an essay. One experiment instead of a whole study plan. Small enough
        that bad weather, literal or otherwise, wasn't an excuse.
      - >-
        And occasionally the rain stops halfway round, the light comes through
        over the Bristol Channel, and you get the reward that only people who
        started anyway get to see.
pullQuote: >-
  The forecast is information, not permission.
