intro: >-
  An Ironman 70.3 is 1.9 km of swimming, 90 km of cycling, and a half marathon,
  one after another. The distance was not the hardest part for me. The hardest
  part was believing, early on, that holding back was not the same as being
  weak.
sections:
  - heading: The first hour lies to you
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        At the start of a long effort everything feels available. Legs are
        fresh, the crowd is loud, and going a little faster seems free. It is
        not free. It is borrowed at a very bad interest rate, and the bill
        arrives on the run, usually somewhere without shade.
      - >-
        Pacing is the discipline of spending energy according to the whole
        distance rather than the current mood. It sounds obvious. It is
        remarkably hard to do while other people are overtaking you.
  - heading: Listening to the boring signals
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        Heart rate, breathing, whether I can still speak a full sentence: none
        of these are exciting, and all of them are more honest than how I feel
        in the moment. Training taught me to trust the dull measurements over
        the dramatic ones.
      - >-
        I notice the same thing at a desk. The urge to sprint through a
        project in its first week is strong and rarely wise. Sustainable pace
        looks unimpressive from the outside and is the only kind that finishes.
  - heading: Eating before you are hungry
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        The best advice I got for endurance events was to eat and drink before
        I needed to. By the time you feel empty, it is too late to fix it
        quickly. It is advice about food, and also about rest, asking for help,
        and taking breaks while they are still optional.
pullQuote: >-
  Going slower at the start is not caution. It is accuracy about how far there
  is to go.
