intro: >-
  In Bangladesh, cha is everywhere: poured at roadside stalls, milky and sweet,
  drunk standing up while a conversation happens around it. Nobody measures
  anything. In my kitchen in Cardiff I own a scale, a timer, and a kettle with
  a thermometer. It took me a while to see these as relatives.
sections:
  - heading: Two very different rituals
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        The tea stall ritual is about people. The drink is almost an excuse, a
        reason to stop for five minutes and talk to whoever is there. It is
        fast, cheap, and unfussy, and it holds a whole neighbourhood together.
      - >-
        Specialty coffee, the way I make it, is solitary and slow. Grind,
        bloom, pour in circles, wait. It is a small experiment every morning,
        and I am mostly the only participant.
  - heading: What they share
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        Both make a clearing in the day. A tea break says "stop for a moment"
        in a busy street; a pour-over says it in a quiet flat. Living far from
        home, I think I brought the need for that clearing with me and simply
        changed the drink.
      - >-
        Sometimes, when the kettle clicks off, I get a flash of a completely
        different place: steam, traffic noise, a glass too hot to hold
        properly. Smell is a terrible filing system and an excellent time
        machine.
  - heading: A small resolution
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        I am trying to make coffee a little more like cha: less about getting
        the extraction perfect, more about making two cups instead of one. The
        scale can stay. The sharing is the part I had forgotten.
pullQuote: >-
  I didn't leave the ritual behind when I moved. I only changed what was in the
  cup.
