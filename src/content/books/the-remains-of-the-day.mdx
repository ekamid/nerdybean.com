author: Kazuo Ishiguro
year: "1989"

thought: A man so devoted to doing his job well that he forgets to notice his own life.
reflection: >-
  Stevens, an English butler, drives across the country and slowly, almost
  against his will, reviews decades of loyal service. The novel is quiet and
  precise, and it works by what the narrator refuses to say. I finished it
  thinking less about butlers and more about how professionalism can become a
  place to hide.
stayed: >-
  The idea that dignity and self-deception can look exactly the same from the
  inside.
margin: >-
  Being excellent at your role is not the same as being present in your life.
  Worth remembering during deadline weeks.
quotes:
  - text: Indeed — why should I not admit it? — at that moment, my heart was breaking.
    note: >-
      The one moment the composure cracks, and it is delivered in the same
      careful, formal voice as everything else. That restraint is what makes
      it devastating.
sections:
  - heading: An unreliable narrator who is very reliable about everything else
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        Stevens remembers the silver polish, the seating plans, the exact
        wording of instructions. What he keeps misremembering, or declining to
        examine, is how he felt. The gap between those two kinds of memory is
        where the whole book lives.
      - >-
        As someone who likes systems and procedures, I recognised the comfort
        he takes in them. Rules are easier than feelings. They also make it
        possible to avoid decisions you should have made yourself.
  - heading: What it left behind
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        A question I now ask about my own work: am I doing this well because it
        matters, or because doing it well lets me avoid thinking about
        something else?
      - >-
        Also a gentler view of regret. The novel does not end in despair. It
        ends with Stevens deciding to make the best of the evening, the
        remains of the day, which is a quietly hopeful place to leave someone.
