status: draft
title: Invisible Cities
author: Italo Calvino
year: "1972"
genre: fable · literary fiction
thought: Every city description is secretly a description of the person describing it.
reflection: >-
  Marco Polo describes impossible cities to Kublai Khan: cities built on
  stilts, cities of memory, cities of desire, cities that exist only as the
  relationships between their inhabitants. It is barely a novel and more like
  a collection of prose poems. I read it slowly, a city or two at a time, and
  kept thinking about the places I have lived.
stayed: >-
  The suggestion that every city Marco describes may really be Venice, seen
  from a different angle, the way every place we visit is filtered through
  home.
margin: >-
  Reading this on a train between Cardiff and somewhere else felt like the
  correct setting.
quotes:
  - text: Cities, like dreams, are made of desires and fears.
    note: >-
      A line I think about whenever a city I love starts to feel different.
      Usually it is me that changed.
  - text: >-
      The city, however, does not tell its past, but contains it like the lines
      of a hand.
    note: >-
      True of Dhaka's crowded streets and of Cardiff's old arcades alike. The
      history is in the corners, not the signs.
sections:
  - heading: A travel book with no travel advice
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        There are no directions, no recommendations, no ten best anything. Each
        city is an idea about how people live together, dressed as a place. It
        made me realise how much of travel writing is really writing about
        attention.
      - >-
        After moving countries, some of these cities felt uncomfortably
        accurate: the city you see for the first time, the city you remember,
        and the city you actually live in are three different places sharing a
        name.
  - heading: What it left behind
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        A habit of writing down one small, specific detail about every new
        place, a sound or smell or colour, rather than trying to summarise it.
        The details last longer than the summaries.
