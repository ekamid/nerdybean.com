title: What I Talk About When I Talk About Running
author: Haruki Murakami
year: "2007"
genre: memoir
thought: A novelist explaining that running is how he learned to write, and to keep going.
reflection: >-
  Murakami writes about training for marathons and a triathlon while also
  writing novels, and about getting older in a body that no longer improves
  on schedule. It is modest, repetitive in a good way, and very honest about
  boredom. I read it while training for longer distances and felt oddly
  accompanied.
stayed: >-
  His insistence that the value of running is not in the results but in the
  consistency: turning up again tomorrow.
margin: >-
  The least motivational book about running I have read, which is exactly why
  it motivated me.
quotes:
  - text: Pain is inevitable. Suffering is optional.
    note: >-
      He presents it as a runner's mantra rather than his own invention. Late
      in a long race, the difference between those two words becomes very
      practical.
  - text: I just run. I run in a void.
    note: >-
      The best description I have found for the part of a long run where
      thinking finally goes quiet.
sections:
  - heading: Discipline without drama
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        There is no transformation story here. He runs, he writes, he gets a
        bit slower with age, he keeps going anyway. The lack of drama is the
        point. Consistency rarely makes a good story, and almost always makes
        a good life.
      - >-
        Training for an Ironman 70.3, I recognised the way long efforts
        simplify you. Most of the time you are not thinking big thoughts. You
        are managing the next few minutes.
  - heading: What it left behind
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        Permission to be an ordinary runner. Not every run has to be faster,
        longer, or meaningful. Most of them just have to happen.
