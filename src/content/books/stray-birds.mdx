title: Stray Birds
author: Rabindranath Tagore
year: "1916"
genre: poetry
thought: Tiny poems that feel like someone leaving notes in the margins of the world.
reflection: >-
  Tagore's name was part of the background of growing up Bangladeshi: his
  songs, his poems, his face on classroom walls. Reading Stray Birds as an
  adult, far from home and in English, felt like meeting a familiar voice in a
  new room. The poems are short, often a single line, and they reward reading
  one at a time.
stayed: >-
  How much can be carried in one sentence if nothing unnecessary is added to
  it.
margin: >-
  A good reminder for someone who writes code comments and commit messages
  for a living: brevity is a form of respect.
quotes:
  - text: Let life be beautiful like summer flowers and death like autumn leaves.
    note: >-
      Gentle and unafraid. It reads less like a statement about death and more
      like advice on how to live.
  - text: >-
      Clouds come floating into my life, no longer to carry rain or usher
      storm, but to add color to my sunset sky.
    note: >-
      I think about this one on grey Cardiff evenings, when the clouds are the
      main event.
sections:
  - heading: Reading home in another language
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        There is something strange about encountering a writer from your own
        culture through translation. Some music is lost; something else,
        distance maybe, is gained. I noticed things I had taken for granted.
      - >-
        These poems were written for anyone, but they carry rivers, monsoons,
        and a particular gentleness that I associate with home.
  - heading: What it left behind
    paragraphs:
      - >-
        A small habit: keeping one line of poetry in my notes app and reading
        it before opening my laptop. It takes ten seconds and changes the
        texture of the morning.
